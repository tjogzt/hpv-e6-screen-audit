### 3.9 GaMD does not support a stable, pose-specific bound state

To ask whether the two nominated candidates form a physically stable bound state rather than a transient surface association, we ran Gaussian accelerated molecular dynamics (GaMD) on the zinc-corrected HPV16 E6 construct used in §3.8. Three independent replicas were launched for CHEMBL848, CHEMBL1129 and the LxxLL positive control from the end structures of the batch-2 20 ns conventional runs, using a GPU-resident GROMACS 2025.4-GaMD build (dual-boost, igamd = 3, σ₀ = 0.25 kcal/mol = 1.05 kJ/mol for both the potential and the dihedral boost). Each replica comprised 0.2 ns of conventional preparation, 2 ns of conventional MD, 0.4 ns of boost preparation and 2 ns of boost equilibration, followed by 100 ns of production with the boost parameters frozen (C-rescale NPT, 300 K, `integrator = md`, `nstcalcenergy = 1`, GPU non-bonded/PME/bonded/update). Replicas ran one per RTX A4000, giving 9 × 100 ns of production in total.

Ligand displacement was measured after superposition on the Cα atoms of the 24 E6AP LxxLL-interface residues rather than on the whole protein, because the interface-bearing domain of E6 moves relative to the rest of the molecule (interface-region Cα RMSD in the global frame: 8.1–18.6 Å across the nine replicas); a global-frame ligand RMSD would therefore conflate ligand drift with protein conformational change, and is reported only for transparency. Contacts were defined per residue as any receptor heavy atom within 4.5 Å of the ligand, and complete detachment as a minimum ligand–receptor heavy-atom distance above 3.5 Å. Occupancies are frame fractions of the boosted ensemble. Boost reweighting was attempted but rejected by pre-specified criteria (β·σ_ΔV up to 1.29, Kish effective sample size 0.2%–3.7% of frames, and a maximum 1.4 kcal/mol disagreement between the second-order cumulant and the exponential-average estimate in 9/9 replicas); no quantitative free-energy profile is therefore reported and every quantity below is reweighting-free.

No system held its docked pose for the whole trajectory, but the three systems failed differently. In the replicas that stayed at the interface (2/3 for CHEMBL848, 2/3 for CHEMBL1129 and 1/3 for LxxLL), the interface-superposed ligand RMSD averaged 6.01–6.07 Å for CHEMBL848, 9.23–11.58 Å for CHEMBL1129 and 8.88 Å for the LxxLL peptide. The remaining replicas left the interface frame (mean RMSD 52.2–75.5 Å) yet, with one exception, stayed in contact with the protein surface, i.e. the association became non-specific rather than breaking. Decomposing the production frames of replicas 1/2/3 into pose retained (RMSD ≤ 4 Å), displaced but in contact, and fully detached gave (8 / 8 / 1) / (92 / 92 / 77) / (0 / 0 / 21) % for CHEMBL848, (0 / 0 / 0) / (100 / 99 / 100) / (0 / 1 / 0) % for CHEMBL1129 and (25 / 0 / 0) / (75 / 100 / 100) / (0 / 0 / 0) % for LxxLL; only CHEMBL848 replica 3 dissociated into solvent.

Engagement of the interface separated the peptide from the two small molecules. Mean occupancy over the 24 interface residues was 0.30 for CHEMBL848, 0.35 for CHEMBL1129 and 0.82 for the LxxLL peptide (Figure 5B). The highest-occupancy residues were VAL53 (0.71), LEU50 (0.71), CYS51 (0.70), LEU67 (0.70), TYR70 (0.70), VAL62 (0.69) for CHEMBL848, ARG131 (0.83), LEU50 (0.79), CYS51 (0.77), LEU67 (0.72), GLN107 (0.66), TYR70 (0.64) for CHEMBL1129 and TYR32 (1.00), LEU50 (1.00), CYS51 (1.00), ARG131 (1.00), TYR70 (1.00), VAL53 (1.00) for LxxLL; all of these lie within the E6AP LxxLL interface. The small molecules therefore do engage the correct groove, but with roughly a third of the per-residue occupancy of the peptide and without preserving their docked orientation.

- **Reading.** GaMD neither rescued the docked poses nor produced an interface-bound population comparable to the LxxLL reference: the peptide occupied the interface 2.8-fold more completely than CHEMBL848 and 2.4-fold more completely than CHEMBL1129, and was the only system to approach its docked orientation for a quarter of a trajectory. Two caveats keep this comparison qualitative: the boost was strong enough to displace even the peptide in two of three replicas, and replica-to-replica spread is large, so no ranking between the two candidates is supported. We therefore read the batch-2 hits as weak, non-specific groove binders whose docking poses are not validated by enhanced sampling. This is consistent with, and reinforces, the negative conclusion of §3.8: the docking/MM-PBSA tier identifies plausible but not kinetically stable starting points, and any experimental follow-up should treat the predicted poses as hypotheses rather than as established binding modes.
