## Abstract

**Background.** No small-molecule E6 inhibitor has reached the clinic. Screens concentrate on the HPV16 E6AP-interface pocket (PDB 4GIZ), and E6 sequence classifiers report >=96% accuracy - yet risk labels are type-level and E6 is phylogenetically structured, so lineage separation masquerades as risk prediction.

**Methods.** We audited 878 E6 records (524 retained, 45 types), clustered types by E6 similarity, and tested 12 physicochemical descriptors at both units under type-grouped cross-validation. We docked 14 ChEMBL ligands into the 4GIZ pocket, audited every start pose, ran three 20 ns MD replicas per complex with DMSO and LxxLL controls (48 runs) under a dual-zinc gate, re-scoring QC-passing complexes with MM-PBSA; an exploratory GaMD check (9 x 100 ns).

**Results.** The dual-zinc scaffold is invariant in all 45 type consensus sequences (27/524 records lack two complete units; no type affected). Ten of twelve descriptors differ across clusters at the type level (largest effect 0.85), but type-grouped discrimination falls to AUC 0.484-1.000 from 0.958-1.000: the descriptors encode lineage, largely within-type. The screen is negative: an initial 42-run batch failed its zinc audit and was withdrawn; in the corrected batch 0/14 candidates are stable under the pre-specified gate, 3/14 reach the positive-control retention range without consistent energetics, and GaMD recovered no docked pose.

**Conclusions.** The contribution is methodological: an audit chain that caught six pipeline defects invisible to the archived pipeline, controls that exposed an over-strict gate, and a quantification of the lineage confound. E6 classifiers on type-level labels are lineage-signature models; single-structure E6 screens need pre-registered structural-validity gates.

**Keywords:** HPV E6; physicochemical features; lineage signature; evolutionary conservation; dual zinc finger; data leakage; confounded labels; MM-PBSA; GaMD; drug screening

## 1. Introduction

E6 and E7 are the two major viral oncoproteins of high-risk human papillomaviruses and cooperate in transformation — E7 neutralising the pRb family, E6 redirecting the E3 ubiquitin ligase E6AP to degrade the tumour suppressor p53 (Werness et al. 1990; Scheffner et al. 1990; Huibregtse et al. 1991). Here we focus on E6, whose E6AP-adaptor function also makes it a principal determinant of type-level oncogenic risk (Moody & Laimins 2010; Vande Pol & Klingelhutz 2013). Pharmacological inhibition of E6 has a two-decade history with a consistently modest ceiling: zinc-ejecting compounds disrupt the E6 zinc fingers but lack selectivity (Beerheide et al. 1999); the first structure-derived virtual screen of E6 — a pharmacophore built from E6-binding peptide structures and screened against the NCI and Sigma-Aldrich collections — yielded five leads active only at 17–29 μM (Baleja et al. 2006); flavonoids identified by structure-based screening against the E6–E6AP interface — starting, as we do here, from the 4GIZ crystal template (Zanier et al. 2013) — disrupt the interaction and stabilise p53 only at low-micromolar concentrations (Cherry et al. 2013); and benzopyranone probes of the E6 helix-binding groove have remained mechanistic tools rather than leads (Rietz et al. 2016). The most recent strategy — covalent inhibitors targeting a cysteine proximal to the E6AP-binding interface, which restore p53 function and suppress HPV16-driven tumours in vivo (Rietz et al. 2026) — has yet to translate clinically, and no E6 inhibitor has reached clinical use.

Separately, machine-learning classifiers of HPV risk from E6 sequence features report near-perfect separation: 97.2% accuracy by leave-one-out cross-validation on 72 types (Kim & Zhang 2006), 96.5% by jackknife on 68 types using reduced-alphabet physicochemical modes (Xu et al. 2020), with ensembled refinements in between (Kim et al. 2009). Two structural facts temper any such figure: risk is a **type-level** attribute, and E6 sequences are phylogenetically structured — a classifier that separates lineages will appear to predict risk.

Structure-based screening against E6 has, in practice, used a single HPV16 structure (4GIZ-derived). This raises two questions that must be kept separate:

- **(B1) The validity premise of single-structure screening.** Does E6 lineage variation localise to the druggable interfaces — the E6AP LxxLL pocket and the p53 interface — rather than being distributed over the rest of the surface? Three readings were specified before the sequence-level analysis was run (and are documented as pre-analysis decisions in the audit log): (i) interfaces differentiate above background, which would invalidate single-structure screening outright; (ii) variation is lineage-structured but does not localise to the interfaces, which makes the single structure an acceptable first-pass approximation; (iii) no lineage structure at all. Reading (ii) is what §3.7b tests, with the explicit caveat that its power (0.28–0.30) reports *not detected* rather than *no difference*.
- **(B2) The screen itself.** Does a single-structure screen of a small candidate set, run with pre-specified structural-validity gates and both controls, produce a reportable verdict either way?

We ran both, plus the lineage analysis that motivates (B1). **The single main contribution of this paper is the audited negative screen of Part B**: an audit chain that caught six pipeline defects, none of which the archived pipeline could have detected itself (Table 1: MD launched from the docking input rather than a pose; start poses never entering the receptor; ligands parameterised without hydrogens, producing a retracted ΔH_PB; a zinc-coordination gate inert through a unit error; a non-bonded zinc model that destroyed the zinc-finger fold; and a first batch run without controls), a negative-control/positive-control pair that recalibrated an over-strict acceptance gate, and a fully negative outcome reported with its exceptions rather than buried. Part A — that physicochemical descriptors of E6 encode lineage identity and that risk labels are untestable at this level — is a secondary, independent cautionary finding that motivates the validity premise; we do not claim that binding is lineage-stratified.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Data source and row-level audit

- Input: `results/10_扩展真实HPV_E6数据集.csv` (878 records, 50 assigned types, 12 physicochemical features).
- Per record: organism species from the `OS=` field; HPV type number from the description string and from the accession suffix.
- Decision rules (recorded per row, nothing deleted): **retain** (human papillomavirus OS, no type disagreement); **exclude** (non-human organism or confirmed type mismatch); **review** (OS missing).
- Script: `rebuild_20261025/code/02_clean_ml_taxonomy.py`.

### 2.2 Feature set

Twelve descriptors: length, molecular weight, aromaticity, instability index, helix/turn/sheet propensity, hydrophobicity (GRAVY), isoelectric point, net charge, positive-/negative-charged residue fractions. Type identity was **not** used as a feature.

### 2.3 Lineage grouping

- One representative (longest) sequence per type; pairwise similarity by `difflib.SequenceMatcher` ratio; distance = 1 − ratio; average-linkage hierarchical clustering, cut at k = 10.
- **Choice of k, and what it does not use.** The clustering input is pairwise E6 sequence similarity only; no official taxonomy information enters the clustering or the choice of k. k = 10 was fixed as the finest resolution at which every cluster still contained a coherent, interpretable set of types, and the taxonomy comparison (§3.2) was run afterwards. The same procedure was re-run at k ∈ {4, 6, 8, 10, 12} and the headline statistics (Kruskal-Wallis significance and effect size, taxonomy purity, leave-one-cluster-out evaluability) are reported for every k (§3.2, §3.4; `code/46_k_robustness.py`).
- Clusters mapped to official taxonomy (de Villiers et al. 2004; ICTV Betapapillomavirus reorganisation; Burk et al. 2013 high-risk superclade alpha-5/6/7/9/11).

### 2.4 Statistical analysis (Part A)

- Per-feature differences across clusters: Kruskal-Wallis; BH-FDR across 12 features; effect size ε². **Two units of analysis are reported for every test**: sequence level (n = 524; historical) and type level (per-type medians, n = 45). Because sequences within a type are near-duplicates, the sequence-level test is pseudoreplicated and the type-level result is the inferential one; both are tabulated (§3.2) and conclusions follow the type-level table.
- Cluster discriminability: one-vs-rest logistic regression (balanced), scaler inside a `Pipeline`, reported under **two fold constructions**: ungrouped stratified 5-fold (historical) and `StratifiedGroupKFold` with groups = HPV type, so that no type is represented in both training and test. n_splits = min(5, number of types in the cluster); grouped CV is undefined for single-type clusters and is reported as such rather than replaced by a within-type number. Clusters with < 5 sequences remain not evaluable.
- Risk-label generalisation: leave-one-cluster-out with the same pipeline; single-class folds recorded as degenerate, not scored. A fold is evaluable only if both the held-out cluster and the remaining training types contain high-risk and non-high-risk types.
- Reference risk classification: IARC Monographs 100B as quoted by the IARC Scientific Council report; type 68 conflict (Group 2A officially; Group 2B/Group 1 in secondary literature) resolved in favour of the official report, conflict documented.

### 2.5 Structural model and dual-zinc coordination quality control (Part B)

- **Receptor (recovered and verified):** a single HPV16 E6 zinc-finger construct, PDB 4GIZ-derived, residues 2–143 (142 residues; template `rec_clean.pdb`) with two structural Zn²⁺ (`zn1.pdb`, `zn2.pdb`, `ZN.mol2`). Structurally, the E6 fold comprises two C4 zinc-binding domains — an N-terminal (E6N) and a C-terminal (E6C) domain — whose juxtaposition forms the E6AP LxxLL-binding pocket that this screen targets; each domain carries one of the two Zn²⁺ ions. The construct ends at PDB residue 143 (UniProt 150) and therefore truncates the final leucine of the C-terminal class-I PDZ-binding motif (ETQ…, lacking UniProt L151), so PDZ-dependent interactions of E6 are outside the scope of this receptor by construction. The E6AP LxxLL peptide is not part of the receptor; it is retained separately (`peptide_LxxLL.pdb`) as the positive control.
- **Measured Zn coordination in the template (re-measured from coordinates on 2026-10-29, with the file-level mapping stated explicitly because it is needed to build the bonded model):** `zn1.pdb` (PDB residue 201, Zn_A) is coordinated by Cys103, Cys106, Cys136 and Cys139 (S–Zn 2.261–2.357 Å); `zn2.pdb` (PDB residue 202, Zn_B) by Cys30, Cys33, Cys63 and Cys66 (2.281–2.358 Å). Cys16 (18.7 / 22.1 Å) and Cys51 (17.2 / 17.4 Å) lie far from both ions and are not coordinating. An earlier internal draft had these two sets swapped; the swap was caught by a coordinate check that aborts if any S–Zn distance exceeds 3 Å (`sanity_check_zn_pairs()`), and it would otherwise have produced 14–18 Å "bonds".
- **Force field and solvation:** protein ff19SB; ligand GAFF2 with AM1-BCC charges (`antechamber -c bcc -at gaff2`, net charge from a verified charge table; `parmchk2` for missing parameters); TIP3P water in a cubic box with 12 Å clearance. Peptide ligands (the E6AP LxxLL positive control) are parameterised with ff19SB standard residue templates instead of GAFF2.
- **Zinc model (final, used in the corrected screen):** a **bonded** ZAFF-type model with deprotonated cysteines. The eight coordinating cysteines are renamed CYM (net charge −1 each), the two ions are placed in the same chain as the receptor (residues 144 and 145) and explicitly bonded to their four SG atoms in tleap (`bond sys.103.SG sys.144.ZN` etc.), with parameters `ZN-SH` (k = 206 kcal mol⁻¹ Å⁻², r₀ = 2.32 Å), `SH-ZN-SH` (k = 27, θ₀ = 109.5°) and Zn 12-6 non-bonded r/2 = 1.10 Å, ε = 0.0125 kcal/mol, charge +2. **This is a reconstructed parameter set, not a verbatim copy of the published ZAFF file** (`zinc_zaff.frcmod` was not recoverable from the remote instance); it is accepted on the basis of the measured Zn–S distance (§2.5b), not on provenance. The resulting system is neutralised automatically by tleap.
- **Zinc model (superseded, used in the first batch):** a 12-6 non-bonded Li/Merz Zn²⁺ model with **neutral** CYS. Simple pairwise 12-6 models of Zn²⁺ are known to reproduce tetrahedral Cys coordination poorly, and bonded or cation-specific dummy-atom approaches are the standard remedy for structural zinc sites (Peters et al. 2010; Li et al. 2013); the first-batch outcome (§2.5b, §3.7) is consistent with that literature, and the model is retained here only because the first batch of 42 runs was produced under it.
- **Per-cluster structures are not built; the lineage question is instead answered by fingerprint-anchored mapping (§2.5c, §3.7b).** Dual-Zn QC and all binding results below refer to the single HPV16 structure; the lineage-stratified comparison of functional sites is done at the sequence level against the experimentally defined interfaces.
- **Acceptance gate (pre-specified for the corrected screen before any batch-2 run; the unit defect in its first implementation is documented in §2.5b):** a run enters the binding analysis only if (i) **both** zinc ions retain tetrahedral four-Cys coordination in ≥95% of production frames (S within 2.8 Å of Zn, ≥4 coordinating S per ion), and (ii) ≥500 of 1001 frames retain the ligand within 4 Å of its docked pose. Runs failing either gate are reported as failed, not silently excluded.
- **What the zinc gate does and does not certify.** Two metrics are reported side by side and are not interchangeable: the *gate criterion* (≥4 S within 2.8 Å in ≥95% of frames, a pass/fail count) and the *continuous geometry metric* (mean distance from each Zn to its four nearest cysteine S atoms, per run). Under the **bonded** model, gate (i) is close to tautological — a covalently bonded Zn–S pair remains within 2.8 Å unless the bond breaks — so "the gate held in 48/48 runs" certifies that the bonds were built and survived, **not** that the site's chemistry is physically correct; that latter judgement rests on the continuous metric against the crystallographic band (0.239–0.241 nm observed vs 0.226–0.236 nm in the template, §3.8) and on the same-seed model comparison of §2.5b. In the superseded non-bonded model the same gate was informative (and failed), which is why it is retained rather than removed.
- **Defect in gate (i) — discovered post hoc, reported, and then fixed.** Inter-atomic distances are returned in nm by the MD analysis library, whereas the 2.8 cut-off was written for Å; the implemented criterion was therefore S within **28 Å**, which is satisfied by essentially every cysteine in a 142-residue protein and does not test coordination at all. The cut-off has been corrected to **0.28 nm** (with an explicit `ZN_S_CUTOFF_ANGSTROM` override and unit annotations in the code). Because the gate was inert in the first batch, the Zn pass rates reported for that batch are **withdrawn** rather than repaired post hoc; the corrected gate is applied from the re-run onward (§2.5b).

### 2.5b Zinc-model selection experiment (Part B)

Because the first batch showed that the receptor's zinc sites were not coordinated at all (Zn–S 0.825–2.372 nm, median 0.997 nm, against 0.226–0.236 nm in the crystal template), the zinc model was chosen by a same-seed comparison under a documented pre-analysis decision (`results/zn_model_selection.md`) rather than by assertion.

- **Design.** The same apo receptor (142 residues + 2 Zn²⁺, TIP3P, 12 Å box, ~31.7 k atoms), identical protocol (EM → 1 ns NVT → 1 ns NPT → 2 ns production) and identical seed (751624) were run for three candidate models, one per GPU: (A) `zaff_cym` bonded ZAFF + CYM; (B) `nonbond_cym` Li/Merz non-bonded Zn²⁺ + CYM; (C) `nonbond_cys` non-bonded Zn²⁺ + neutral CYS, i.e. the first-batch model, run as the failure baseline.
- **Criterion (documented pre-analysis decision, recorded in `results/zn_model_selection.md` before the comparison was run).** PASS if, over the final 50% of frames, the mean distance from each Zn to its four nearest cysteine S atoms is ≤ 0.30 nm and the frame maximum ≤ 0.35 nm. The metric is the same one recorded per run in the main screen, so the two are directly comparable.

| Model | Zn1 (nm) | Zn2 (nm) | Both (nm) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A `zaff_cym` (bonded ZAFF + CYM) | 0.2392 | 0.2385 | **0.2388** | **PASS — adopted** |
| B `nonbond_cym` (non-bonded + CYM) | 0.1982 | 0.1987 | 0.1984 | PASS but 0.03–0.04 nm **too short** |
| C `nonbond_cys` (first-batch model) | 1.0127 | 1.0534 | 1.0331 | **FAIL** |

- **Outcome.** Model A is adopted: 0.2388 nm sits at the upper edge of the crystallographic range (0.226–0.236 nm) and is stable throughout (max 0.2422 nm). Model B passes the numeric threshold but compresses the site by ~0.3 Å relative to the template — a "passes the gate but physically wrong" outcome that we do not adopt. Model C reproduces the first-batch failure exactly, and its **first frame is already 0.807 / 1.005 nm**, i.e. the ion leaves the coordination shell during minimisation/equilibration rather than drifting slowly; the first-batch damage was therefore a receptor-modelling defect, not a sampling artefact.
- Scripts: `code/24_zn_model_test.py` (build + MD), `code/26_zn_analyse.py` (analysis); selection record `results/zn_model_selection.md`; per-model output `zn_model_test/<model>/zn_result.json`.

### 2.5c Mapping functional sites onto every type without homology models (Part B)

Cross-type sequence identity to the HPV16 template is low (0.26–0.49 for the cluster representatives; §3.2). Building and comparing ten homology models would therefore flood any binding comparison with modelling error, so we instead mapped the **experimentally defined functional sites** back onto every E6 sequence and tested whether lineage differentiation concentrates at those sites.

- **Site definitions from crystal coordinates, not from literature memory.** From 4GIZ we computed the E6AP LxxLL interface (E6 chain C residues whose heavy atoms lie within 5.0 Å of the fused LxxLL motif `LTLQELL`, fusion-chain residues 373–379) and the two Zn-coordination spheres (Cys SG within 3.0 Å of Zn). From 4XR8 (the E6–E6AP–p53 ternary complex; 4GIZ itself contains **no p53**) we computed the p53 interface (E6 chain F/H heavy atoms within 5.0 Å of the p53 DBD chains C/D). All coordinates were re-measured locally; nothing was imported from a secondary source. **Numbering.** 4GIZ chain C is numbered 2–143 and corresponds to UniProt P03126 (VE6_HPV16) residues 9–150 per the DBREF record (PDB residue n = UniProt residue n + 7, cross-checked against SEQADV); the fused E6AP motif (fusion-chain residues 372–383) corresponds to UBE3A (Q05086) residues 403–414. All interface positions used in §3.7b and Figure 4 are reported in UniProt numbering, converted with that offset (`GIZ_TO_UNP_OFFSET` in `code/29_define_interface_positions.py`). **Both templates are mutated at the same four positions**: 4GIZ carries F47R + C80S/C97S/C111S/C140S, and 4XR8 retains the C80S/C97S/C111S/C140S set (verified against SEQADV records). C97S (3.2 Å) and C111S (4.6 Å) lie inside the p53 interface, so the p53-interface definition rests on constructs in which those positions are serine in **both** templates; no E6–p53 structure with wild-type cysteines exists. This affects side-chain identity at two interface positions, not the geometric definition of the interface (which is what §3.7b uses); Cys97 and Cys111 are non-coordinating (§2.5), so the zinc-site results are unaffected.
- **Fingerprint-anchored mapping.** A global pairwise alignment slips at <50% identity — it reported HPV18 as lacking a coordinating Cys that the sequence actually contains (Cys138/Cys141; the alignment had shifted the residue off-register). We therefore anchored each sequence by its own **zinc-finger fingerprint** (two `C-X-X-C ⋯(25–35)⋯ C-X-X-C` units), which is a hard structural constraint, and piecewise-linearly interpolated positions between the eight anchors. A forced self-check aborts if any anchored position is not a cysteine.
- **Selection-free verification of the conservation claim.** Anchoring selects on the motif it is meant to detect: a sequence that lacks the fingerprint cannot anchor, so the self-check can never observe a non-cysteine at an anchor. The conservation statement is therefore tested independently, without the fingerprint as a gate (`code/47_selection_free_zinc_scan.py`): for every one of the 878 input records (all retain/exclude/review decisions), all C-X-X-C pairs are enumerated over every cysteine combination and all candidate sites over every pair combination, with a site defined as two pairs whose inter-pair loop is 25–35 residues; a record passes if it contains two non-overlapping sites in N-to-C order. The same scan reproduces the 27 anchoring failures of the mapping exactly, and any record that fails while being long enough to contain both fingers is reported as a record-level exception rather than dismissed (§3.7b).
- **Unit of analysis and background.** To avoid pseudoreplication, the unit is the **type** (n = 45), not the sequence: each type contributes one consensus residue per position (the modal residue across its anchored sequences). For each position we computed **lineage coherence** = P(two types of the same cluster share the residue) − P(two types of different clusters share it). Functional-site groups were compared against a **background** of all non-functional positions in UniProt 30–150 by Mann-Whitney U with a permutation p (9999 permutations, seed 42), and 3-way BH-FDR. Post-hoc power is reported so that a "not significant" result is not misread as "no difference".
- **Engineered-mutation caveat.** The 4GIZ template carries five engineered mutations (F47R, C80S, C97S, C111S, C140S). Three lie **inside the p53 interface** (F47R at 3.5 Å, C97S at 3.2 Å, C111S at 4.6 Å); none lies in the E6AP LxxLL pocket that the ligand screen targets (nearest F47R at 10.1 Å). F47R is absent from 4XR8, which is the relevant alternative template. This caveat is stated wherever the p53 interface is referenced.
- Scripts: `code/28_coord_cys_diagnosis.py`, `code/29_define_interface_positions.py`, `code/31_lineage_structure_v2.py`; outputs `results/29_interface_summary.json`, `31_type_consensus.csv`, `31_functional_vs_background.json`.

### 2.6 Molecular docking and start-pose validity (Part B)

- **Candidate-set provenance (reconstructed from surviving artifacts and reported as such).** The 14 candidates were not selected by an E6-specific rule, and we describe how they were obtained rather than rationalise them post hoc. A 74-compound library was built from the ChEMBL web API (`molecule/search` endpoint, accessed 2025-10-02; the ChEMBL release number was not recorded) using 20 indication keywords ("antiviral", "antineoplastic", "oncology", "antiinfective", "HPV", "papillomavirus", "cancer", "tumor", "neoplasm", "chemotherapy", "oncolytic", "antitumor", "antineoplastic agents", "virus inhibitor", "viral", "DNA virus", "E6", "p53", "PDZ"), deduplicated by canonical SMILES, filtered by Lipinski and Veber criteria, and diversified greedily by Morgan fingerprint (radius 2, 2048 bits, maximum pairwise Tanimoto < 0.8) — script `02_fetch_ligand_library.py`, output `ligands/01_candidate_ligand_library.csv`. **The 14 candidates are the first 14 entries of that library file, taken in file order** — a positional truncation, evidenced by the docking progress log (`results/molecular_docking/docking_progress.json`, 2025-10-07) and the surviving per-ligand artifacts; no score-, activity- or structure-based selection rule was recorded at the time, and none is claimed now. The set consists of named, largely marketed or clinically investigated antiviral and antineoplastic agents (e.g. aciclovir, vidarabine, trifluridine, tipiracil, pleconaril, trimetrexate, lenalidomide, medroxyprogesterone acetate, futibatinib, pirtobrutinib), three investigational compounds (disoxaril, irofulven, nolatrexed) and the carbamate insecticide carbaryl; none has documented activity against E6. The full per-ligand table (ChEMBL ID, name, SMILES, computed properties, Lipinski/Veber status, Vina score) is generated from the primary artifacts by `code/49_candidate_provenance.py` into `results/49_candidate_provenance_table.csv`; nothing in it is transcribed by hand. The consequence for scope is stated in §4: the negative verdict is a statement about this candidate set under this protocol, not about ChEMBL antivirals or E6-druggability at large.
- 14 ChEMBL candidate ligands; AutoDock Vina (exhaustiveness 8, 9 poses per ligand, energy range 4.0 kcal/mol). Best Vina scores ranged from −5.21 to −7.84 kcal/mol.
- **Start-pose validity audit (completed; three defects found and corrected), audit table `results/20_start_pose_engagement_audit.csv`:**
  1. *Un-docked starting structures.* The archived pipeline used `docking_results_full/4GIZ/<ligand>.pdb`, which is the docking **input**, not a pose: centroid displacement relative to the Vina reference pose was 63.3–77.6 Å (median 72.1 Å; script `13_prepare_vina_poses.py`, `results/13_vina_poses.csv`). All runs now start from Vina poses converted with Open Babel.
  2. *Missing hydrogens.* Vina poses use a polar-hydrogen model, so the pose PDBs carried 0–3 hydrogens on 15–34 heavy atoms. Ligands are now protonated at pH 7.4 (`obabel -h -p 7.4`) before parameterisation; the net formal charge of each protonated ligand was then verified independently (RDKit valence perception after clearing the mol2 charge column): **all 14 ligands are neutral at pH 7.4** (`charge_table.csv`; source recorded per run).
  3. *Poses that never entered the receptor.* For 3 of 14 ligands the best-scoring pose made no contact with the receptor (minimum heavy-atom distance 9.16 / 10.05 / 9.47 Å for CHEMBL119 / CHEMBL717 / CHEMBL848, no residue within 6 Å, ≥15 Å from either Zn). A uniform rule was applied (documented as a pre-analysis decision after the audit found the defect) — take the best-ranked pose whose minimum heavy-atom distance to the receptor is ≤3.6 Å — replacing these three with their MODEL 2 pose (3.12–3.36 Å; Vina score penalty 0.02–0.14 kcal/mol). After correction **14/14 poses are engaged** (2.86–3.45 Å). Original MODEL 1 files are retained for audit.

### 2.7 MD protocol, pose-retention filter and MM-PBSA re-scoring (Part B)

- **Simulation:** GROMACS; energy minimisation (steepest descent, 5 000 steps), 1 ns NVT equilibration (v-rescale, 300 K), 1 ns NPT equilibration (C-rescale, 1 bar), then 20 ns production (dt = 2 fs, v-rescale 300 K, Parrinello–Rahman 1 bar, PME, 1.0 nm cut-offs, h-bonds constrained), saving every 20 ps (1001 frames). Three independent replicas per ligand, each with a deterministic seed derived from ligand name and replica index (42 runs in batch 1; 48 runs in batch 2, which adds the DMSO and LxxLL controls).
- **Pose-retention filter:** after stripping solvent, protein backbone (CA, C, N, O) is fitted to the first production frame and the ligand heavy-atom RMSD is computed without further fitting; frames with RMSD < 4 Å are retained. The reference structure is thus the **first production frame**, not the docked pose itself; any drift accumulated during equilibration is invisible to this metric, and ligand drift during the NVT/NPT phases was not separately recorded (the trajectories reside on a decommissioned instance), so the retained fractions are upper bounds on pose survival from the docked pose. A run is scored only if ≥500 of 1001 frames are retained.
- **MM-PBSA:** MMPBSA.py, PB-only (`istrng = 0.15`, `inp = 1`, `radiopt = 0`, `fillratio = 4.0`), every 5th frame (201 frames per run). We report the PB interaction **enthalpy** ΔH_PB; no entropic term was computed, so these values are not binding free energies and are interpreted only as relative, conditional on pose retention.
- **Parser correction (completed):** the stock summary prints `DELTA TOTAL = 0.0000` for all 14 candidates; the true quantity is the sum of VDWAALS + EEL + INTERNAL + EPB + ENPOLAR + EDISPER from the Differences block. A strict parser (`05_fix_candidate_energy_parser.py`) raises on a missing block; regression against the archived summary passes 14/14 (tolerance 0.01 kcal/mol).

### 2.8 Controls and GaMD validation (Part B)

- **Controls (included in batch 2, completed).** Positive: the E6AP LxxLL peptide in its crystallographic position on the E6 surface, parameterised with ff19SB. Negative: DMSO, placed at the centroid of the docked-pocket reference ligand, parameterised with GAFF2 like the candidates. We attach **no external kcal/mol expectation** to the positive control — its measured ΔH_PB under this protocol (≈−115 kcal/mol, §3.8) is a peptide-sized PB enthalpy, not a binding free energy, and should not be compared with small-molecule ΔG values; the control's function is purely empirical, to define the retention scale (floor 0.215) and the rescue-consistency check. Both controls run through the identical pipeline, gates and parser; in the 2 ns pilot the peptide retained 96/101 frames within 4 Å and DMSO 1/101, and in batch 2 they separate cleanly at full length (retained fraction: DMSO ≤0.005 vs LxxLL ≥0.215 in every replica; §3.8). That the peptide itself fails the strict 500-frame hard gate in 1 of 3 replicas is internally consistent with the μM-scale affinities typical of E6–LxxLL-class interactions and with a 20 ns observation window: borderline stability at this timescale is exactly what a control is for.
- **GaMD (completed).** GROMACS 2025.4-GaMD (v2.1), GPU-resident, `integrator = md`, dt = 2 fs, C-rescale NPT at 300 K, `nstcalcenergy = 1`, PME; dual boost (`igamd = 3`) with σ₀ = 0.25 kcal/mol (1.05 kJ/mol) for both the potential and the dihedral boost, and `irest_gamd = 0`. Each replica: ntcmdprep 0.2 ns + ntcmd 2 ns + ntebprep 0.4 ns + nteb 2 ns (2.3 × 10⁶ steps), then 100 ns of production with the boost parameters frozen (52 × 10⁶ steps total). Three replicas per system for CHEMBL848, CHEMBL1129 and the LxxLL positive control (9 × 100 ns), one replica per RTX A4000. Frames were written every 10 ps and coordinates made whole and continuous (`gmx trjconv -pbc mol`, then `-pbc nojump`) before analysis. Boost reweighting was pre-specified (criteria fixed in `code/43_gamd_reweight.py` before the trajectories were analysed) to be reported only if it passed (β·σ_ΔV ≤ 1.5, Kish effective sample size ≥ 15% of frames, and ≤ 1 kcal/mol agreement between the second-order-cumulant and exponential-average PMF estimators); it passed in 0/9 replicas, so no free-energy profile is reported and §3.9 uses reweighting-free observables only.
- **Cluster-stratified comparison is not pursued.** A binding-energy comparison across lineage clusters would require per-cluster receptor models, which are not built (cross-type identity 0.26–0.49 would confound it with modelling error). The lineage question is instead answered at the sequence level in §3.7b.

### 3.7 Dual-zinc stability and pocket QC (Part B — HPV16 structure)

- **The dual-Zn gate did not function in the first batch (see Methods 2.5).** The implemented cut-off was 28 Å rather than 2.8 Å, so the reported pass rates (formally 37/42 runs ≥95%, mean 0.909) are uninformative and are **withdrawn**. The diagnostic that does carry information is the mean distance from each Zn to its four nearest cysteine S atoms: 0.825–2.372 nm (8.3–23.7 Å; median 0.997 nm) in that batch, against 0.226–0.236 nm (2.26–2.36 Å) in the crystal template. **The zinc sites were therefore not tetrahedrally coordinated in the first batch**; the five runs that fell below even the inert threshold (0.176–0.299) are cases where the ion separated still further.
- **Fixed and verified (§2.5b).** With the bonded ZAFF + CYM model and the 0.28 nm cut-off, the same metric is **0.239–0.241 nm (2.39–2.41 Å)** across all 48 corrected runs, with 100% of frames passing the coordination gate. The zinc defect is therefore resolved.
- **Consequence.** Because the zinc-finger fold is what forms the pocket, the zinc defect was a sufficient explanation for the loss of docked poses in batch 1; both defects (ligand protonation, §3.6b, and zinc) are now fixed, and batch 2 (§3.8) tests binding on the corrected receptor.
- **Pocket/pose retention gate:** 6 of 42 runs (14.3%) retained ≥500 of 1001 frames within 4 Å of the docked pose; one of them (CHEMBL320775, replica 1, retention 0.797) also fell below the zinc threshold, so 5 runs (11.9%) passed both gates. Median pose-retention across all 42 runs was 0.137 (mean 0.224, maximum 0.847).
- **The corrected screen (batch 2) is complete**: bonded ZAFF + CYM, gate cut-off 0.28 nm, 16 ligands × 3 replicas = 48 runs, controls included. The zinc gate held in all 48 runs (Zn–S 0.239–0.241 nm). §3.8 below reports batch 2; the batch-1 table is retained in the audit trail (archived) and is not interpreted.
- **Zinc-site stability is not lineage-dependent.** This is now answered by §3.7b without per-cluster models: all eight coordinating cysteines are invariant in every one of the 45 type consensus sequences, so the coordination chemistry that forms the pocket does not vary by lineage. (A dynamics-level test would still require the per-cluster cohort, but the sequence-level evidence removes the mechanistic basis for the hypothesis.)

### 3.7b The dual-zinc scaffold is invariant in every type consensus sequence; functional interfaces do not show elevated lineage differentiation

Instead of building ten homology models, we mapped the experimentally defined functional sites onto every type by fingerprint anchoring (§2.5c) and asked whether lineage differentiation concentrates at them.

- **The dual-zinc scaffold is invariant in every type consensus sequence.** All eight coordinating cysteines (UniProt 37, 40, 70, 73, 110, 113, 143, 146) are cysteine in the consensus sequence of **every one of the 45 types**, spanning the alpha-, beta- and mu-papillomavirus genera. Because every type has the same residue at these positions, the lineage-coherence metric is undefined there (within- and between-cluster identity are both 1.0) — the sites carry no lineage signal at all. This holds under the sequence-fingerprint definition (§2.5c), and the geometry of the template is consistent with four-cysteine coordination (Zn–S 2.26–2.36 Å); a single-type crystal structure cannot by itself confirm conservation across 45 types. The conservation itself is not a new finding — the eight cysteines were established by the original HPV16 genome annotation (Cole & Danos 1987) — and our contribution is its quantitative, selection-free confirmation across 45 types.
- **Selection-free check, and what it bounds.** The fingerprint procedure cannot in principle observe a non-cysteine at its own anchors, so we tested the claim independently (§2.5c). Scanning all 878 input records without the fingerprint as a gate (`code/47_selection_free_zinc_scan.py`): 497 of the 524 retained records contain two complete CXXC–CXXC units (loop 25–35), and every one of the 45 types has at least one such sequence, so the type-level statement is supported. Nine retained records (1.7%) do not contain two complete units and are reported rather than excluded: six have unknown-residue (X) runs within five residues of a cysteine, i.e. the "loss" is an annotation gap rather than an observed substitution (HPV33 tr|A0A159DR32, four HPV35 records, HPV58 tr|G5CCY9, HPV30 tr|A0A161D377), and three are clean single-cysteine deviations in otherwise complete sequences (HPV31 tr|G5CD47, Cys103 position absent; HPV35 tr|A0A6M3SAE6; HPV81 tr|D7NSZ7, one cysteine of the second finger absent). The claim is therefore stated at the level it survives — *invariant across the 45 type consensus sequences* — and not as invariance of every deposited record.
- **Mapping quality.** 497 of 524 sequences anchored to the fingerprint; all 27 failures are TrEMBL entries (0 Swiss-Prot) and shorter than the retained set (131.4 ± 15.3 aa vs 152.1 ± 8.3 aa). The selection-free scan (§2.5c) reproduces these 27 exactly, and shows that 9 of them are long enough to contain both fingers (143–154 aa) and are therefore record-level exceptions rather than fragments; the remaining 18 are C-terminally truncated before the second finger. Exclusion is not selective by type: each of the 45 types retains at least one two-site sequence and no type loses its consensus.

### 3.8 Corrected screen of 14 ChEMBL candidates with both controls (Part B — batch 2)
- Batch 2 re-runs the same 14 candidates plus a negative control (DMSO) and a positive control (E6AP LxxLL peptide) on the zinc-corrected receptor: 3 replicas × 20 ns each, **48/48** runs completed.
- **The zinc gate now holds.** Across all runs the mean Zn–S(nearest 4) distance is 0.240 nm (range 0.239–0.241 nm; crystal template 0.226–0.236 nm), i.e. the bonded ZAFF/CYM model keeps both zinc sites tetrahedral for the whole trajectory — the defect of §3.7 is fixed, not merely relaxed.
- **The two controls separate cleanly and give the screen a scale.** DMSO never stayed in the pocket (retained-frame fraction ≤0.005; 0/3 replicas pass QC; no ΔH reported), whereas the LxxLL peptide retained ≥0.215 of frames in every replica (0.215 / 0.570 / 0.688; 2/3 pass QC; ΔH_PB = −115.38 ± 0.53 kcal/mol, per-replica −115.75 / −115.01). The gap between the two controls (0.210) is the empirical yardstick used below.
- **Tier scheme (post-hoc, calibrated on the controls).** The pipeline's pre-specified hard rule (a replica passes if it retains ≥500/1001 frames **and** the zinc-gate is ≥0.95) turned out to be mis-calibrated relative to the positive control: the LxxLL peptide itself fails it in 1 of 3 replicas (0.215 < 0.499). We therefore also report control-calibrated tiers, defined *after* inspecting the controls and disclosed as such: a run is *DMSO-like* if its retained fraction is ≤0.050 (twice the negative-control ceiling, with a lower bound of 0.05), *LxxLL-like* if it is ≥0.215 (the positive-control floor), and *intermediate* in between; ligands are tiered by the median of their three replicas.
- **Uncertainty and circularity of the tier thresholds (quantified).** Both thresholds are extremes of n = 3 control replicas: the floor is the *minimum* of {0.215, 0.570, 0.688} and the ceiling the *maximum* of {0.001, 0.004, 0.005}. A nonparametric bootstrap over the three control replicas (100 000 resamples, seed 42; `code/50_tier_floor_bootstrap.py`) puts a 95% interval of **[0.215, 0.688]** on the floor — i.e. a re-drawn control trio could set the LxxLL-like threshold as high as the highest replica — and a 29.7% probability on a floor ≥ 0.5. Two consequences are stated explicitly. First, **replica-to-replica differences within a ligand do not constitute ranking evidence**: the spread among the control's own replicas (0.215–0.688) exceeds the spread between many candidates, so per-replica values are shown but no candidate-to-candidate ordering within a tier is claimed. Second, the controls serve simultaneously as judge and rule-maker: the same three LxxLL runs define both the floor and the reference behaviour the floor is meant to capture, so the tiers are a *relative* calibration internal to this campaign, not transferable thresholds. A campaign intending quantitative tiering should run enough control replicas (n ≥ 5–10) to bound the floor's sampling error before using it as a decision boundary.
- **The hard gate and the controls disagree, and the controls win the argument.** For CHEMBL848 all three replicas stay above the positive-control floor yet none reaches 500/1001 frames, so the hard rule reports it as UNSTABLE with no ΔH — while the positive control itself fails the same rule in 1 of 3 replicas. On the control-calibrated reading, CHEMBL848 is the best-retained small molecule in the set. Its ΔH_PB was therefore missing under the original protocol (MM-PBSA was run only on hard-QC-passing replicas); we recovered it offline below, without re-simulation.
- **A fragility specific to CHEMBL848's hit status.** CHEMBL848 is one of the three ligands whose best-scoring (MODEL 1) docked pose never engaged the receptor (9.47 Å; §2.6), so every result reported for it — its above-floor retention, its rescued ΔH_PB, its GaMD nomination — derives from the MODEL 2 pose admitted by the post-audit ≤3.6 Å engagement rule rather than from its best-scoring pose. ### 3.9 Exploratory GaMD check: the docked poses are not recovered (evidence grade: qualitative)

To ask whether the two nominated candidates form a physically stable bound state rather than a transient surface association, we ran Gaussian accelerated molecular dynamics (GaMD) on the zinc-corrected HPV16 E6 construct used in §3.8. Three independent replicas were launched for CHEMBL848, CHEMBL1129 and the LxxLL positive control from the end structures of the batch-2 20 ns conventional runs, using a GPU-resident GROMACS 2025.4-GaMD build (dual-boost, igamd = 3, σ₀ = 0.25 kcal/mol = 1.05 kJ/mol for both the potential and the dihedral boost). Each replica comprised 0.2 ns of conventional preparation, 2 ns of conventional MD, 0.4 ns of boost preparation and 2 ns of boost equilibration, followed by 100 ns of production with the boost parameters frozen (C-rescale NPT, 300 K, `integrator = md`, `nstcalcenergy = 1`, GPU non-bonded/PME/bonded/update). Replicas ran one per RTX A4000, giving 9 × 100 ns of production in total.

Ligand displacement was measured after superposition on the Cα atoms of the 24 E6AP LxxLL-interface residues rather than on the whole protein, because the interface-bearing domain of E6 moves relative to the rest of the molecule (interface-region Cα RMSD in the global frame: 8.1–18.6 Å across the nine replicas); a global-frame ligand RMSD would therefore conflate ligand drift with protein conformational change, and is reported only for transparency. Contacts were defined per residue as any receptor heavy atom within 4.5 Å of the ligand, and complete detachment as a minimum ligand–receptor heavy-atom distance above 3.5 Å. Occupancies are frame fractions of the boosted ensemble. Boost reweighting was attempted but rejected by the pre-specified criteria (β·σ_ΔV 1.11–1.29, within the 1.5 ceiling in all nine replicas; Kish effective sample size 0.15%–3.7% of frames, below the 15% floor in all nine; cumulant-versus-exponential-average disagreement above 1 kcal/mol in three of nine, up to 1.40); no replica passed all three criteria, so no quantitative free-energy profile is reported and every quantity below is reweighting-free.

No system held its docked pose for the whole trajectory, but the three systems failed differently. In the replicas that stayed at the interface (2/3 for CHEMBL848, 2/3 for CHEMBL1129 and 1/3 for LxxLL), the interface-superposed ligand RMSD averaged 6.01–6.07 Å for CHEMBL848, 9.23–11.58 Å for CHEMBL1129 and 8.88 Å for the LxxLL peptide. The remaining replicas left the interface frame (mean RMSD 52.2–75.5 Å) yet, with one exception, stayed in contact with the protein surface, i.e. the association became non-specific rather than breaking. Decomposing the production frames of replicas 1/2/3 into pose retained (RMSD ≤ 4 Å), displaced but in contact, and fully detached gave (8 / 8 / 1) / (92 / 92 / 77) / (0 / 0 / 21) % for CHEMBL848, (0 / 0 / 0) / (100 / 99 / 100) / (0 / 1 / 0) % for CHEMBL1129 and (25 / 0 / 0) / (75 / 100 / 100) / (0 / 0 / 0) % for LxxLL; sustained dissociation into solvent occurred only in CHEMBL848 replica 3 (21% of frames), and every other replica was fully detached in ≤1% of frames.

Engagement of the interface separated the peptide from the two small molecules. **Aggregation convention:** a residue's occupancy for a given replica is the fraction of that replica's production frames in which any heavy atom of the residue lies within 4.5 Å of the ligand; a residue's occupancy for a system is the mean over its three replicas; the headline numbers are the mean over the 24 E6AP LxxLL-interface residues. Occupancies are therefore **not** conditioned on the ligand remaining at the interface — frames in which the ligand is displaced elsewhere on the surface or detached contribute as zero — so they measure time-averaged engagement of the specific groove, and the identity of the highest-occupancy residues (below) is what carries the "correct groove" reading, not the absolute values. Mean occupancy over the 24 interface residues was 0.30 for CHEMBL848 (replica means 0.394 / 0.402 / 0.100), 0.35 for CHEMBL1129 (0.428 / 0.247 / 0.368) and 0.82 for the LxxLL peptide (0.829 / 0.823 / 0.814) (Figure 5B). The highest-occupancy residues were VAL53 (0.71), LEU50 (0.71), CYS51 (0.70), LEU67 (0.70), TYR70 (0.70), VAL62 (0.69) for CHEMBL848, ARG131 (0.83), LEU50 (0.79), CYS51 (0.77), LEU67 (0.72), GLN107 (0.66), TYR70 (0.64) for CHEMBL1129 and TYR32 (1.00), LEU50 (1.00), CYS51 (1.00), ARG131 (1.00), TYR70 (1.00), VAL53 (1.00) for LxxLL; all of these lie within the E6AP LxxLL interface. The small molecules therefore do engage the correct groove, but with roughly a third of the per-residue occupancy of the peptide and without preserving their docked orientation.

- **Reading — qualitative, exploratory, and not confirmatory.** Under this protocol the peptide control occupied the interface 2.8-fold more completely than CHEMBL848 and 2.4-fold more completely than CHEMBL1129, and was the only system to approach its docked orientation for a quarter of a trajectory; the two small molecules did engage the correct groove, with roughly a third of the peptide's per-residue occupancy and without preserving their docked orientation. Two design features limit what this can mean, and both cut against reading it as evidence about the ligands: (i) the boost was strong enough to displace the peptide positive control itself in two of three replicas, so a ligand that fails to hold its pose cannot be distinguished from a protocol that is too aggressive; and (ii) every replica was launched from the terminal frame of a 20 ns conventional trajectory — a structure in which the docked pose had already drifted — so the starting ensemble is biased against pose retention for all three systems. We therefore report this as a qualitative stability check: the docked poses are not recovered under an over-strong protocol from drifted start structures, which is consistent with the weak, non-specific binding read in §3.8 but does not independently establish it. Distinguishing "weak binder" from "over-strong protocol" would require replicas started from the docked pose, or a boost calibrated so that the peptide control retains its pose; neither was run, and the underlying trajectories are no longer available (they were lost with the compute instance), so this remains an open item rather than a result.

## 4. Discussion

**Part A — what physicochemical descriptors of E6 actually encode.** The physicochemical profile of E6 is a strong marker of lineage: charge-related descriptors and hydrophobicity separate the clusters with large effect sizes (type-level ε² 0.77–0.85 for the four leading descriptors), the clusters remain largely discriminable one-vs-rest when no type is shared between training and test (AUC 0.91–1.00 for four clusters, but 0.48–0.75 for three others; a fifth cluster reaches 0.991 on only two types and carries no interpretable precision, §3.3), and they are concordant with official taxonomy (7/10 clusters pure; clusters 1 and 2 jointly recover the published alpha-5/6/7/9/11 superclade, with cluster 2 alone covering alpha-9/6/5/11). This is compatible with — but much weaker than — risk prediction: because the risk label is defined at the type level and types are phylogenetically structured, the two explanations are statistically indistinguishable, and the leave-one-cluster-out test that would separate them is degenerate at every resolution (one or zero evaluable folds out of 4–12). The correct reading is therefore not "physicochemical descriptors predict oncogenic risk" but "physicochemical descriptors encode lineage identity", and any E6 classifier trained on type-level labels should be reported as a lineage-signature model — with the caveat that even the lineage signal is largely a within-type signal unless evaluation is grouped by type.

**Part B — the single-structure screen is a negative result, and the corrected receptor confirms it.** On the zinc-corrected HPV16 E6 construct (batch 2, 48 runs), none of the 14 ChEMBL candidates maintained its docked pose across three independent 20 ns replicas under the pre-specified gate (0 STABLE, 2 MARGINAL, 12 UNSTABLE). Against the controls (DMSO ≤0.005 vs LxxLL ≥0.215 retained fraction), only 3/14 candidates reach the positive-control range of pose retention (CHEMBL848 0.363/0.381/0.303, CHEMBL235668 0.559/0.347, CHEMBL1129 0.657/0.272 above the floor). Rescue scoring of above-floor replicas that the gate excluded (same PB protocol, no re-simulation; exploratory) shows that energetics, not only retention, discriminate the candidates: CHEMBL848 is the only candidate with three consistent favourable replicas (−15.91 / −19.54 / −19.72; mean −18.39 ± 2.15 kcal/mol), CHEMBL1129 is consistent across two (−9.24 / −8.36), whereas CHEMBL235668 flips sign (+4.34 / −5.18) — the latter is the empirical demonstration that these ΔH_PB are not binding free energies. Docking scores were uniformly modest (−5.2 to −7.8 kcal/mol) and did not predict MD survival: the best Vina score in the set (CHEMBL4650485, −7.84) retained at most 30% of frames in one replica, while the best-retained candidate (CHEMBL848, −6.70) ranked fourth. The pattern is consistent with weak, non-specific surface association for most of the set, with CHEMBL848 as the single follow-up-worthy signal; we report all ΔH_PB as conditional on pose retention, not as binding free energies. The negative verdict is also consistent with the field's two-decade experience: the best E6 inhibitors reported to date act only at low-micromolar concentrations, whether zinc ejectors (Beerheide et al. 1999), pharmacophore-screened leads at 17–29 μM (Baleja et al. 2006), or interface-targeted flavonoids docked, as here, from the 4GIZ template (Cherry et al. 2013) — our MD-level criterion is stricter than the biochemical assays behind those leads, so a weaker verdict is expected a priori. The scope of the verdict is bounded by the candidate set (§2.6): the 14 compounds are the leading entries of an indication-keyword ChEMBL library, not an E6-enriched or diversity-maximised set, and none has documented E6 activity — so the "3/14 reach the positive-control retention range" figure should be read against a background of unenriched, mostly marketed antiviral and antineoplastic drugs, and the verdict is a statement about this set under this protocol, not about E6 druggability in general.

**The audit chain is, in our view, the substantive contribution of Part B.** Six pipeline defects were found, plus one template-provenance caveat (ledger above); three of the defects had gone entirely unnoticed and none would have been detected by the archived pipeline itself: (i) three of fourteen start poses never entered the receptor (9.16–10.05 Å from the nearest receptor atom) because the extracted MODEL 1 was used without an engagement check; (ii) every ligand was parameterised without hydrogens, so a naphthalene system was typed as non-aromatic GAFF2 `c1`/`c2` — the same single-variable A/B test that fixed this also showed that the previously circulated ΔH_PB of −14.58 ± 0.63 kcal/mol for CHEMBL46917 was an artefact, and that value is retracted; (iii) the dual-Zn gate that was supposed to guarantee tetrahedral Cys coordination never constrained anything, because a 2.8 cut-off written for Å was evaluated against distances in nm. The recorded Zn–S separations (0.825–2.372 nm, median 0.997 nm, versus 0.226–0.236 nm in the template) show that the non-bonded Zn²⁺ model with neutral cysteines does not preserve the zinc-finger fold — which is a sufficient structural explanation for the widespread loss of docked poses, and the reason we treat every binding number in §3.8 as provisional.

**Interpretation and what remains untested.** The corrected re-run now resolves the ambiguity left by batch 1: on a receptor whose zinc sites demonstrably hold, binding is genuinely weak — 3/14 candidates reach the positive-control retention range, none under the pre-specified gate in 3/3 replicas, and none with reproducible favourable energetics. Two qualifications remain. First, the verdict is about *this protocol* (20 ns replicas, MM-PBSA on retained frames). We also ran an exploratory GaMD check on the three positive-control-range candidates (§3.9, 9 × 100 ns) and it recovered no docked pose, but that check cannot settle the question: the boost was strong enough to displace the peptide positive control in two of three replicas, and every replica started from the terminal frame of a 20 ns trajectory rather than from the docked pose, so "not recovered" is as consistent with an over-strong protocol as with a genuine absence of a stable bound state. We therefore treat it as a qualitative observation and do not count it as support for the screen verdict; a protocol-calibrated test (docked-pose start, or a boost that preserves the peptide control) would be needed to make that comparison evidentiary. Second, the pre-specified 500-frame gate proved mis-calibrated relative to the positive control (which fails it in 1/3 replicas); we therefore report control-calibrated tiers alongside it, and we would not reuse the 500-frame rule without first re-deriving it from controls in any new campaign.

- **Mechanistic payoff — partly answered at the sequence level, in the direction of outcome (ii).** The lineage signature in sequence space does **not** concentrate at the druggable interfaces: the dual-zinc scaffold is invariant in all 45 type consensus sequences (§3.7b), and neither the E6AP LxxLL nor the p53 interface shows lineage differentiation above background (rank-biserial +0.22, p = 0.17–0.18, power 0.28–0.30, so this is *not detected*, not *no difference*). This is reading (ii) specified in §1: physicochemical variation is lineage-structured, but it does not localise to the pocket — which is consistent with the 4GIZ single-structure screen being an acceptable *approximation* for a first pass, and is not offered as a demonstration that interface structure is invariant. The claim survives in a narrower form — *what is conserved across lineages is the zinc-finger scaffold, and what varies is the rest of the surface* — not as *binding is lineage-stratified*. The dynamics-level counterpart (pocket breathing, zinc-site stability per cluster) still requires the per-cluster structural cohort, which is not built.
- **How the position-level nulls sit with HPV biology.** The E6AP-interface null is biologically unsurprising: E6AP binding is the defining, type-spanning function of E6 — the interaction was established for the canonical high-risk types (Werness et al. 1990; Huibregtse et al. 1991) and the LxxLL groove is the conserved structural solution for it — so a *geometric* interface defined on the HPV16 template having no excess lineage variation above background is what one expects for a function shared by all types. The p53 side requires more care: high- and low-risk E6 proteins differ functionally in p53 degradation efficiency, yet the p53-interface positions do not differentiate above background either. Three non-exclusive explanations are compatible with both facts: (a) functional divergence may be driven by a small number of positions whose individual contribution is too weak for this test at power 0.28–0.30 (MDE r_rb ≥ 0.43); (b) divergence may act through dynamics and affinity rather than residue identity — the same residues with different conformational stability; or (c) divergence may reside partly outside the static geometric interface, e.g. in determinants of E6AP recruitment or in the cellular context of expression. Our data do not distinguish among these, and we make no claim that p53-interface sequence is identical across risk classes — only that positional identity does not differentiate beyond background at this power.
- Practical consequences: type-level E6 classifiers should be reported as lineage-signature models; E6 docking campaigns should pre-register structural-validity gates — start-pose engagement, ligand protonation and valence verification, and dual-Zn coordination QC — because three of these failure modes occurred unnoticed in the archived pipeline, and two of them (defects 2 and 4) had already produced publishable-looking numbers.

## 5. Limitations

**Part A.**
- Clusters are data-derived but show strong concordance with official taxonomy (7/10 pure; clusters 1 and 2 jointly recover the published HR superclade, cluster 2 alone covering alpha-9/6/5/11).
- Risk labels are type-level proxies; no variant-level functional or clinical outcome data.
- 321 records excluded and 33 held for review reduce the effective sample to 524; several clusters are small (n ≤ 11; three not evaluable for ungrouped AUC, two undefined under grouped CV).
- **Within-type pseudoreplication.** The 524 sequences are near-duplicates within types (cluster 2 holds 355 records from 11 types). Every test is therefore reported at both units (§3.2) and conclusions follow the type-level (n = 45) table; the sequence-level p-values should not be read as evidence from 524 independent observations.
- **Grouped CV changes the picture.** With no type shared between training and test, one-vs-rest AUC falls to 0.484–1.000 (from 0.958–1.000 ungrouped) and two clusters sit at or below 0.61 (§3.3). The ungrouped values should be regarded as an upper bound that includes within-type repetition.
- **k is a choice, not a result.** k = 10 was fixed before the taxonomy comparison and the headline statistics are stable across k ∈ {4, 6, 8, 10, 12} (§3.2, §3.4), but purity rises with k by construction (more clusters, smaller ones), so the purity figure should not be read as an independent validation of k = 10.
- IARC evaluation covers 18/45 types, of which 15 agree with the dataset label under a Group 1 vs non-Group-1 split; 27 types are unevaluated and keep dataset labels as working labels only (§3.5).
- Sequence-similarity clustering uses `difflib` ratios rather than a proper alignment-based distance; this is adequate for lineage-scale separation but is not a phylogenetic reconstruction.

**Part B.**
- **Single structure.** All 42 runs (batch 1) and the 48 corrected runs (batch 2) used one HPV16 construct (4GIZ-derived, residues 2–143). The lineage question is now answered at the sequence level by fingerprint-anchored mapping (§3.7b) rather than by per-cluster models, but that answers only *where* the variation is; a dynamics-level comparison (e.g. zinc-site stability or pocket breathing per cluster) would still require the structural cohort, which is not built.
- **Engineered mutations in the template.** The 4GIZ receptor carries five engineered mutations (F47R, C80S, C97S, C111S, C140S); three — F47R, C97S and C111S — lie **inside the p53 interface** (3.5, 3.2 and 4.6 Å, respectively). 4XR8, the alternative template, retains the same 4C/4S set (C80S/C97S/C111S/C140S, with C97S and C111S again inside the p53 interface), so **no E6–p53 structure with wild-type cysteines at those positions exists**; this is a template-provenance limit on any p53-interface inference, not a pipeline defect. The substitutions do not lie in the E6AP LxxLL pocket that the ligand screen targets (nearest is F47R at 10.1 Å), so the screening verdicts are not directly affected, but any inference touching the p53 interface is made on a construct that is not wild-type there. No sensitivity analysis against 4XR8 has yet been run.
- **Fingerprint-anchored mapping, and its verification.** The lineage comparison (§3.7b) uses a zinc-fingerprint anchor and piecewise-linear interpolation, not an alignment; it is reliable inside the zinc-finger core but should not be read as a precise side-chain assignment in loops far from the anchors. Because the anchor is the motif under test, the conservation claim was re-tested with a selection-free scan of all 878 records (§2.5c): 45/45 types retain at least one sequence with two complete CXXC–CXXC units, but 9 of the 524 retained records (1.7%) lack one unit — six at unknown-residue gaps, three clean single-cysteine deviations in TrEMBL entries (§3.7b). The claim is therefore made for type consensus sequences, not for every deposited record.
- **Power of the lineage test.** The functional-vs-background comparison has post-hoc power 0.28–0.30 and can only detect rank-biserial effects ≥ 0.43–0.46 (§3.7b). "Not detected" must therefore not be read as "no difference"; a small true differentiation would be invisible at 45 types.
- **Zinc model (resolved, with a declared caveat).** The archived ZAFF file could not be recovered, so a **reconstructed** bonded ZAFF-type parameter set with deprotonated CYM is used (§2.5). It reproduces the crystallographic Zn–S distance (0.2388 nm vs 0.226–0.236 nm in the template) and was selected by a same-seed three-way comparison (§2.5b) rather than by provenance, but it is not a verbatim copy of the published ZAFF file, and no sensitivity analysis against a second independent zinc parameterisation has been run. This should be stated as a parameter-set uncertainty in any binding claim.
- **Zinc gate implementation defect (resolved).** The gate cut-off was written as 2.8 Å but evaluated against nm distances, i.e. 28 Å, so it never rejected anything in batch 1. The formal pass statistics from that batch are withdrawn rather than repaired post hoc; the cut-off is now 0.28 nm and the gate is active from batch 2 onward.
- **Uncorrected ligand-protonation history.** The 14 ligand topologies were rebuilt at pH 7.4 for this batch, but the retracted pilot value (§3.6b) shows the archived pipeline had run without hydrogens; any downstream artefact circulating from that pipeline is unverified until re-run.
- **Replica dispersion.** Three 20 ns replicas per complex are sufficient to demonstrate irreproducibility but not to converge a free energy; ΔH_PB is reported with the number of contributing replicas and is not interpreted as ΔG_bind.
- **Controls (batch 1 lacked them; batch 2 includes them).** Batch 1 had no control runs, so its verdicts were uncalibrated. The E6AP LxxLL peptide (positive) and DMSO (negative) ran in batch 2 and separate cleanly at full length (DMSO ≤0.005 vs LxxLL ≥0.215 retained fraction; §3.8). Two limits remain: the peptide control is parameterised with ff19SB, not GAFF2, so its MM-PBSA values are not directly comparable in absolute terms to the small-molecule candidates; and the control-calibrated tiers were defined after inspecting the controls (disclosed in §3.8), so they are post-hoc, not pre-specified.
- **GaMD is exploratory only.** The 9 × 100 ns GaMD check started from the terminal frames of 20 ns trajectories and used a boost that displaced the peptide positive control itself in 2/3 replicas, so it cannot distinguish "no stable bound state" from "protocol too aggressive"; boost reweighting failed the pre-specified criteria in every replica, so no free-energy profile is available. A protocol-calibrated replicate (docked-pose start, or a boost under which the peptide control keeps its pose) was not run, and the raw trajectories were lost when the compute instance was released, so this item can only be closed by a new simulation campaign.
- **No experimental validation.** No candidate has been tested biophysically or in cells; the strongest supported claim available is about pose retention, not affinity.
- **Exploratory rescue of ΔH_PB.** For replicas that exceed the positive-control floor but fall below the 500-frame gate, ΔH_PB was re-computed offline on the same retained frames (no re-simulation; §3.8). These values are more favourable-biased by construction (frames are selected for pose stability) and are used only to rank replica consistency, not to claim binding; the positive control's rescued replica (−110.24) sits 4.5% from its gate-scored replicas (−115.75/−115.01), which bounds the frame-count sensitivity of the estimate.
- **Candidate-set provenance.** The 14 candidates are the leading entries of a ChEMBL indication-keyword library, taken in file order, with no E6-focused selection rule recorded (§2.6). The verdict's external validity is bounded accordingly: it speaks to this set under this protocol.
- **Pose-retention reference and equilibration drift.** Retained fractions are measured against the first production frame, not the docked pose, and ligand drift during equilibration was not separately recorded, so the reported fractions are upper bounds on survival from the docked pose (§2.7).
- **Pose substitution for three ligands.** CHEMBL119, CHEMBL717 and CHEMBL848 entered the screen through the post-audit ≤3.6 Å engagement rule (MODEL 2 poses), not their best-scoring poses; CHEMBL848's status as the leading signal is conditional on that documented substitution (§3.8).

**Box 1 — A reusable audit checklist for single-structure docking-to-MD screens.**
Each item below was learned as a defect or near-miss in this campaign; we offer it as the transferable core of Part B.

1. **Start-pose engagement.** Before any simulation, verify that the extracted pose contacts the receptor (minimum heavy-atom distance ≤ ~3.5 Å, threshold fixed in advance). Selecting a pose by rank alone admitted three poses 9–10 Å from the receptor.
2. **Ligand protonation and valence.** Protonate at the assay pH and verify the net formal charge independently of the parameterisation tool. Unprotonated input silently mistyped an aromatic system and produced a retracted ΔH.
3. **Metal-site QC with unit-annotated cut-offs, plus a continuous metric.** For a metalloprotein, gate on coordination with the unit written next to the number (Å vs nm — a 10× error made our gate inert), and report a continuous geometry metric against the crystallographic band; under a bonded model the pass/fail gate alone is close to tautological.
4. **Controls through the identical pipeline, and tiers calibrated on them — with their sampling error.** Run positive and negative controls through every gate and parser. If a control fails a pre-set gate, report the disagreement rather than silently relaxing the gate; when thresholds are derived from control replicas, state n and the threshold's uncertainty (ours: floor 0.215, bootstrap 95% CI [0.215, 0.688] at n = 3).
5. **Parser strictness.** Analysis code should fail loudly on missing blocks or unexpected zeros; a permissive parser printed `DELTA TOTAL = 0.0000` for all 14 candidates.
6. **Contingency when controls fail.** If the positive control does not separate from the negative, the screen has no scale: report it as uncalibrated, do not tier candidates, and fix the protocol before re-running. If the positive control passes only marginally (as ours did in 1/3 replicas), report both the pre-set gate and the control-calibrated reading, and never let a post-hoc rule silently replace the pre-set one — disclose the substitution and its direction of bias.
